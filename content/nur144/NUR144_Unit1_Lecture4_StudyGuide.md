## ANATOMY AND PHYSIOLOGY

### Normal stool

**75% fluid, 25% solid.** The brown color comes from **breakdown of bile by gut bacteria.**

> That's why biliary obstruction gives **clay-colored** stool — no bile pigment reaching the gut.

### Elimination

**Rectal distention** causes contraction of the musculature and **relaxes the INTERNAL anal sphincter**. The **EXTERNAL** anal sphincter is under **voluntary** control. Contracting the abdominal muscles aids defecation.

> Continence needs three intact parts: **muscle** (the sphincters), **nerve** (the signal), and **brain** (the decision). Damage anywhere on that path causes incontinence.

### Gases

Absorbed by the **portal circulation**, **detoxified by the liver**, or expelled from the rectum as **flatus**.

### The gut microbiome — four functions

**Breakdown of waste** · **vitamin synthesis** · **immune functions** (both protective and inflammatory) · colonization begins **at birth** and is established by **age 2**

> **The GI tract is sometimes called "your other nervous system"** — there is an enormous amount of nerve activity controlling it, and **mind and emotions** play a real role in function. Stress makes some people constipated and others diarrheal.
>
> Antibiotics can wipe out the microbiome for months.

## LABS AND DIAGNOSTICS

**Eight studies for the lower GI tract:**

Serum labs · **STOOL TESTS** (occult blood, pathogenic bacteria, **ova and parasites**) · ultrasound · **genetic testing** (e.g., Hirschsprung) · imaging: CT, PET, MRI, scintigraphy, **virtual colonoscopy** · **lower GI tract study (barium enema)** · **GI motility studies** · **sigmoidoscopy and colonoscopy**

> Stool tests look for **pathogenic** bacteria specifically, since normal stool is full of bacteria anyway. "Ova and parasites" is exactly what it sounds like.

**BARIUM ENEMA** — barium instilled **rectally**, then imaged. Shows the **shape** of the colon, where the barium goes, and strictures or masses.

> If a patient has both an upper endoscopy and a colonoscopy the same day, the **endoscopy goes first.**

**How far does each scope go?**

| Scope | Reaches |
|---|---|
| **COLONOSCOPY** | Through rectum and sigmoid into the **descending, transverse and ascending** colon — the whole colon |
| **SIGMOIDOSCOPY** | Past the proximal sigmoid into the **descending colon only** |

**Eight tests for MALABSORPTION:**
Fat analysis · **lactose tolerance test** · **D-xylose absorption test** · **Schilling test** (B12) · **hydrogen breath test** · endoscopy with biopsy · ultrasound, CT, radiography · CBC and pancreatic function tests

> Each targets one nutrient path. The **Schilling test** checks B12 absorption — connecting to intrinsic factor from Lecture 1. The **hydrogen breath test** works because humans don't make hydrogen; bacteria do. Hydrogen means carbohydrate reached bacteria unabsorbed.

## CONSTIPATION

**Fewer than three bowel movements weekly**, or stools that are **hard, dry, small or difficult to pass**. Presents with distention, bloating, **straining**, and a sensation of **incomplete evacuation**.

**PERCEIVED constipation** — subjective; the pattern doesn't match what the person believes is normal.

> The textbook gives a fixed number; in lecture she taught it **relative to the patient's own baseline**. A patient who normally goes three times a day and hasn't gone in two days *is* constipated, even though two days is normal for many people.
>
> About **15% of Americans** have chronic constipation (symptoms ≥3 months). Most is **idiopathic**.

**In older adults:** 24–50% have chronic constipation. 10–18% of community-dwelling and up to **74% of skilled nursing residents** use laxatives daily. The most common complaint is **the need to strain**.

### Nine causes

Many **medications** · chronic **laxative** use · weakness · **immobility** · fatigue · inability to increase **intra-abdominal pressure** · diet · **IGNORING THE URGE** · lack of regular exercise

**Two medications she named specifically: OPIOIDS and IRON.**
> With opioids, **start a bowel regimen EARLY** — don't wait for the constipation to happen. Opioids slow motility; iron hardens stool.

**Why ignoring the urge is a vicious cycle:** hold it in and the colon keeps pulling water out — *like playdough left out overnight*. The stool comes out **hard and it hurts**. So next time they hold it longer.

### Six complications

**DECREASED CARDIAC OUTPUT** · fecal **IMPACTION** · **HEMORRHOIDS** · **FISSURES** · **RECTAL PROLAPSE** · **MEGACOLON** (dilated, atonic colon)

> **Cardiac output leads the list** because **straining is a Valsalva maneuver** — it raises intrathoracic pressure, reduces venous return, and drops cardiac output. Dangerous in patients with **stroke risk or cardiac disease**.

**The three mechanical ones:**
- **HEMORRHOIDS** — varices in the rectal area. *"Think esophageal varices, but in the rectum."*
- **FISSURE** — a longitudinal **tear** in the anal canal lining, usually from passing a large firm stool. Painful defecation, burning, **bright red blood on the tissue.** Treated conservatively: fiber, stool softeners, water, **sitz baths**.
- **PROLAPSE** — an organ that should be up has come **down**, pushed out by gravity, straining and intra-abdominal pressure. Also uterine and bladder prolapse.

**FECAL IMPACTION WITH OVERFLOW** — a hard mass sits in the rectum and fresh liquid stool squeezes **around** it.
> **This is constipation presenting as diarrhea.** Treating it as diarrhea makes it worse. May need manual disimpaction.

### The gastrocolic reflex

Food entering the stomach signals the colon to **make room** — *"hey colon, we're sending some luggage down."*

**What comes out is the PREVIOUS meal**, not what was just eaten.

> **Use it:** teach the patient to attempt a bowel movement **at the same time each day**, ideally **after a meal** or a warm drink. Morning coffee does the same thing. This is the basis of a bowel training program.

### The Bristol Stool Chart

A **1–7 scale**. **Type 1** — hard separate lumps (*"popping pinballs"*). **Type 4** — smooth, soft, sausage-shaped: **IDEAL**. **Type 7** — liquid diarrhea.

> Not on the slides, but she flagged it. Give it to the patient and let them point.

### Laxative classes

| Class | Example | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **BULK-FORMING** | Psyllium | Adds fiber mass |
| **LUBRICANT** | Mineral oil, glycerin suppository | Softens the hard plug |
| **STIMULANT** | **BISACODYL**, senna | Increases **peristalsis and mucus** |
| **EMOLLIENT** | **DOCUSATE** | **Hydrates** the stool |
| **OSMOTIC** | **POLYETHYLENE GLYCOL** | **Pulls water in** |
| **SALINE** | **Magnesium hydroxide** (milk of magnesia) | |

> ⚠️ **Your SYLLABUS lists docusate as a stimulant — that is an ERROR.** Docusate is an **emollient** stool softener. **Bisacodyl** is the stimulant. Both the slide and the textbook agree.

> Mucus is *"a very good slippery medium"* — stimulants work on both movement and lubrication.

**In long-term care:** facilities **track bowel movements aggressively** with a standing regimen (e.g., no BM in 2 days → start MiraLax). It's a big problem there because of **immobility and reduced cognition.**

### HIRSCHSPRUNG DISEASE

Other name: **CONGENITAL AGANGLIONIC MEGACOLON** — and the name is the whole disease.

- **CONGENITAL** — born with it
- **AGANGLIONIC** — no nerve ganglia to drive peristalsis in that segment
- **MEGACOLON** — the result: stool backs up like a dam and the colon dilates

> Detected by genetic testing. A clue in a child: *"my child clogs the toilet all the time."*

## DIARRHEA

**More than three bowel movements per day with increased liquidity.** May come with urgency, perianal discomfort, incontinence, nausea, or dehydration.

| Category | Duration |
|---|---|
| **ACUTE** | Self-limiting, **up to 14 days** |
| **PERSISTENT** | **14–30 days** |
| **CHRONIC** | **More than 30 days**, may return sporadically |

> Acute and persistent are usually **infectious** — bacterial (E. coli, C. difficile) or viral (rotavirus, norovirus), with **viral most common**. Drugs also cause them: erythromycin, magnesium-containing antacids.
>
> **Chronic** has a different cause list — chemotherapy, antiarrhythmics, antihypertensives, metabolic and endocrine disease. Over a month, stop looking for an infection.

### Classification

**ACUTE, by inflammation:**
- **NONINFLAMMATORY** — **large** volume, **noninvasive** pathogens
- **INFLAMMATORY** — **small** volume, **invasive** or toxin-producing pathogens

> **Volume is the discriminator.** A noninvasive pathogen sits in the lumen and makes the gut secrete — large watery volume. An invasive one damages the wall, so volume is small but there is **blood, mucus and fever**.

**CHRONIC, five mechanisms:**

| Mechanism | What happens |
|---|---|
| **SECRETORY** | Mucosa secretes water and electrolytes (bacterial toxins, chemo); high volume |
| **OSMOTIC** | Unabsorbed particles **pull water in** (lactase deficiency, pancreatic dysfunction) |
| **MALABSORPTIVE** | Nutrients not absorbed; low albumin swells the mucosa |
| **INFECTIOUS** | Organisms invade the mucosa |
| **EXUDATIVE** | Mucosal damage from radiation or chemotherapy |

> **OSMOTIC** connects to **lactose intolerance** and **dumping syndrome** — same water-follows-solute physics.
>
> **C. difficile** is the most common bacterium in antibiotic-associated diarrhea.

### Presentation

Increased frequency and fluid content · abdominal **cramps** · distention · **BORBORYGMUS** · anorexia and thirst · painful spasmodic contractions of the anus · **TENESMUS**

- **BORBORYGMUS** = rumbling, gurgling bowel sounds
- **TENESMUS** = the painful, ineffective urge to defecate — *the feeling you have to go, but nothing comes out*

### Complications and what to avoid

**COMPLICATIONS** — fluid and electrolyte imbalances · **DEHYDRATION** · **CARDIAC DYSRHYTHMIAS** · irritant dermatitis from chronic diarrhea

> **Dysrhythmias** because you lose potassium with the water. This is the same chain as ileostomy output.

**AVOID** — caffeine · carbonated beverages · **very hot and very cold** foods. May need to avoid milk, fat, whole grains, fresh fruit and vegetables.

## FECAL INCONTINENCE

### Causes — grouped by which part of the continence system failed

| System | Causes |
|---|---|
| **MUSCLE** | Sphincter weakness: traumatic (rectal surgery) or nontraumatic (scleroderma); pelvic floor disorders (prolapse) |
| **NERVE** | Neuropathies: peripheral (pudendal) or generalized (diabetes) |
| **BRAIN** | CNS disorders: dementia, stroke, spinal cord injury, MS |
| **BOWEL** | Inflammation (radiation proctitis, IBD); diarrhea; **fecal impaction with overflow** |
| **BEHAVIOR** | Behavioral disorders |

### Four treatments

**Bowel training program** · **skin care** · fecal management system for acute illness · **EMOTIONAL SUPPORT**

> Emotional support is listed as **treatment**, not courtesy — this is among the most distressing symptoms in nursing.

**Diagnosed with:** history, rectal exam, endoscopy, radiography, barium enema, CT, and **anorectal manometry**

## IRRITABLE BOWEL SYNDROME (IBS)

A **CHRONIC FUNCTIONAL** disorder — recurrent abdominal pain with disordered bowel movements (diarrhea, constipation, or **both**), with **NO identifiable cause**.

- Prevalence **4.4–4.8%** of US adults
- **Females affected 1.5–2× more**
- Usually diagnosed **under age 50**
- May be caused by **neuroendocrine dysregulation** leading to **altered peristalsis**

> **"FUNCTIONAL" is the key word** — the bowel **looks normal** on every test. The problem is how it *moves* and how *sensitive* it is, not what it's made of.
>
> Diagnosed under 50 matters: new bowel symptoms in an **older** patient are not assumed to be IBS, because cancer and diverticular disease become likely.

**Assessment:** alteration in bowel patterns · pain · bloating · abdominal distention

**Diagnosis:** stool studies, contrast radiography, proctoscopy, barium enema, colonoscopy, manometry, electromyography — **all to rule things OUT** — then **ROME IV criteria** to rule IBS **IN**.

> Every test comes back normal. **That is the diagnosis.** IBS is what's left when structural disease is excluded.

**Eight teaching points:**
Medication management · complementary medicine · dietary changes · **FOOD DIARY** · adequate fluid intake · avoid alcohol and smoking · **RELAXATION TECHNIQUES** · **LOW FODMAP diet**

> The food diary matters because triggers are **individual**. Relaxation is **treatment**, not comfort — stress is a named trigger and the mechanism is neuroendocrine.

## MALABSORPTION

The inability of the digestive system to absorb one or more major vitamins, minerals or nutrients.

**HALLMARK** — diarrhea, or frequent **loose, bulky, foul-smelling stools with high fat content**, often **grayish** (steatorrhea)

### CELIAC DISEASE

An **AUTOIMMUNE** disease with a **hereditary** component that **damages the VILLI**.

**Treated with a STRICT GLUTEN-FREE DIET FOR LIFE**, with dietary consult and support.

**Presents with:** diarrhea, **steatorrhea**, constipation, failure to thrive, distention, **poor muscle tone**, irritability, **dental disorders**, **anemia**, **delayed puberty** or amenorrhea, nutritional deficiencies

> **Risk of OSTEOPOROSIS** because calcium and vitamin D aren't absorbed.
>
> Villi exist only in the **small intestine** — which is why celiac and Crohn's (distal ileum) cause malabsorption, but ulcerative colitis (colon only) does not.
>
> Recovery is slow: the textbook says a **full year** of gluten-free eating before villous integrity is restored.

### LACTOSE INTOLERANCE

A deficiency of **LACTASE** — the enzyme that digests **lactose**, the sugar in milk. Undigested lactose stays in the bowel and **pulls water in osmotically**, causing **osmotic diarrhea** and cramps after milk products.

**Diagnosed by:** positive **lactose tolerance test** or **hydrogen breath test**

**Five teaching points:**
1. Eliminate milk and milk products
2. Watch for **HIDDEN milk** in processed foods (dried milk as filler)
3. Pretreat with **lactase drops** (Lactaid), or take a lactase tablet **with the first bite**
4. Most people tolerate **1–2 cups daily** in small amounts spread through the day
5. **YOGURT with active cultures** digests lactose better than lactase preparations

> **The risk of eliminating dairy:** milk is a major source of **calcium and vitamin D**, so cutting it without supplements leads to **osteoporosis** — same risk as celiac, same reason.

## PERITONITIS AND THE ACUTE ABDOMEN

**ACUTE ABDOMEN** — also "surgical abdomen": acute onset of pain **WITHOUT TRAUMA** requiring **swift surgical intervention**

**PERITONITIS** — inflammation of the peritoneum, usually **bacterial**. **RIGID BOARDLIKE abdomen.**

**Three types:**

| Type | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **PRIMARY** | Spontaneous infection, often with **LIVER FAILURE** |
| **SECONDARY** | Follows **PERFORATION** of an abdominal organ |
| **TERTIARY** | Superinfection in an **IMMUNOCOMPROMISED** patient |

> **Primary** with liver failure is spontaneous bacterial peritonitis — ascitic fluid becomes a culture medium and the Kupffer cells that would filter the bacteria are bypassed.

**Diagnosed with:** WBC/CBC, x-ray/CT/MRI, and **peritoneal aspiration** with culture and sensitivity

**Treatment:** supportive care and **control the source** · fluids · pain medication and antiemetics · **ANTIBIOTICS: large dose, broad spectrum**

**Monitor closely for:** change in bowel function and for **SEPSIS**

> **Broad spectrum** because you're treating gut flora spilled into a sterile cavity and can't wait for cultures.

### ⚠️ THE RIGID BOARDLIKE ABDOMEN — four conditions in this unit

Perforated **ULCER** · perforated **APPENDIX** · severe **PANCREATITIS** · ruptured **GALLBLADDER**

**It always means PERITONITIS.** The muscles clamp involuntarily over an inflamed peritoneum. Surgical emergency regardless of source.

## DIVERTICULAR DISEASE

A **DIVERTICULUM** is a **sac-like herniation** of the bowel lining extending through a defect in the **muscle layer**.

| | Definition |
|---|---|
| **DIVERTICULOSIS** | Multiple diverticula **WITHOUT** inflammation |
| **DIVERTICULITIS** | **INFECTION AND INFLAMMATION** of diverticula |

**Most common in the SIGMOID COLON** — *not* the ileum. Increases with **age** and is associated with a **LOW-FIBER DIET**. Usually diagnosed by **colonoscopy**.

### Presentation by severity

| Stage | Findings |
|---|---|
| **MILD** | Bowel irregularity with **ALTERNATING constipation and diarrhea**, nausea, anorexia, bloating/distention |
| **WITH INFLAMMATION** | Acute onset of mild to severe **CRAMPING PAIN IN THE LLQ** · constipation · bloating with nausea · **FEVER** |
| **WITH ABSCESS** | All of the above **PLUS** bleeding · tenderness · **PALPABLE MASS** · **PERITONITIS** |

### Four interventions

Encourage fluid intake of **at least 2 L/day** · **SOFT foods with INCREASED FIBER**, such as cooked vegetables · individualized **exercise** program · **BULK LAXATIVES** (psyllium) and **stool softeners**

> ⚠️ **Colonoscopy is CONTRAINDICATED in acute severe diverticulitis** — insufflating air into inflamed bowel risks perforation.

## INTESTINAL OBSTRUCTION

Blockage preventing the normal flow of intestinal contents.

| Type | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **MECHANICAL** | Intraluminal obstruction, or **MURAL** obstruction from pressure on the intestinal wall |
| **FUNCTIONAL / PARALYTIC** | The intestinal **musculature cannot propel** contents. May be temporary, from **manipulation of the bowel during surgery** |

### PARALYTIC ILEUS

The bowel **stops moving**. The body doesn't know it was a clean surgical knife — *"it thinks it was gored"* — so the gut **shuts down to protect itself**, keeping contents from moving through an opening into the peritoneal space.

> This is why you listen for bowel sounds every shift after GI surgery, why the NG tube stays until they return, and why **"passed gas yet?"** is the question.

**ADHESIONS** are scar tissue that grows together after abdominal surgery. **Years later** they can cause small bowel obstruction. **Lysis of adhesions** is the surgery to break them up.

> She said you don't need "lysis of adhesions" for the exam — but **prior abdominal surgery as a RISK FACTOR** for obstruction matters.

### Small vs. large bowel obstruction (textbook)

| | **SMALL BOWEL** | **LARGE BOWEL** |
|---|---|---|
| **Pain** | **Crampy, wavelike, colicky** | Develops **slowly** |
| **Vomiting** | **Yes** — peristalsis eventually reverses toward the mouth | Late |
| **Dehydration** | **Rapid** | Slower |
| **Stool** | Blood and mucus but **no stool or flatus** | **Constipation** may be the only symptom for weeks; altered stool shape |
| **Other** | The **lower** the block, the **more** distention | **Iron-deficiency anemia** from blood loss; eventually marked distention with visible bowel loops |

**Why small bowel obstruction causes SHOCK:**
Contents, fluid and gas accumulate above the block → distention → pressure rises → **THIRD-SPACING** of fluid, electrolytes and protein into the lumen → circulating volume falls → **HYPOVOLEMIC SHOCK**

**The electrolyte picture from vomiting:** loses hydrogen ions and potassium → **HYPOCHLOREMIA, HYPOKALEMIA, METABOLIC ALKALOSIS**

> Large bowel dehydrates slower because the colon can absorb its contents and distend well beyond normal. **Both end in perforation, peritonitis and sepsis if untreated.**

> **80% of obstructions occur in the SMALL intestine**; adhesions, hernia and tumor account for 90% of those. Most large bowel obstructions are in the sigmoid: cancer **60%**, diverticular disease **20%**, volvulus **10%**.

### Treatment and resolution

**TREAT** — **DECOMPRESSION** with an NG tube, IV fluids and electrolyte correction. Surgery if decompression fails or the bowel is strangulated.

**RESOLVING** — return of **normal bowel sounds** · decreased abdominal distention · subjective improvement in pain and tenderness · **PASSAGE OF FLATUS OR STOOL**

> **Passage of flatus is the single most useful sign** — it means something got all the way through.

## INFLAMMATORY BOWEL DISEASE

A group of **chronic** disorders: **CROHN'S DISEASE** (also called regional enteritis) and **ULCERATIVE COLITIS**.

### CROHN'S DISEASE

Subacute and chronic inflammation of the GI tract wall through **ALL LAYERS**. Most commonly in the **DISTAL ILEUM and ASCENDING COLON**.

**Progression:** inflammation → **abscesses** → **ulcers and scarring**. Creates a **COBBLESTONE** appearance, **SKIP LESIONS**, **narrowing** of the lumen from thickened walls, and **adhesions**.

**Three findings specific to Crohn's:**
**PERIANAL involvement** · **FISTULAS** · **ABDOMINAL MASS**
Plus weight loss, malnutrition and **anemia from FOOD AVOIDANCE**

> **A FISTULA is an abnormal connection between organs that should not be connected.** Crohn's causes them because full-thickness inflammation **tunnels** to a neighboring structure.

### ULCERATIVE COLITIS

Chronic ulcerative and inflammatory disease of the **MUCOSAL AND SUBMUCOSAL** layers of the **COLON AND RECTUM**.

**Progression:** periods of remission and exacerbation, typically **starting in the RECTUM** and progressing **proximally**. **CONTINUOUS** lesions.

**Four findings specific to UC:**
**BLOODY or PURULENT diarrhea** — bleeding is from the **ulcerations** · **LLQ pain** · pallor, **anemia**, fatigue · anorexia, weight loss, fever, vomiting

**TOXIC MEGACOLON** can occur. **Probiotics** may be part of treatment.

> **"When someone tells you they have bloody diarrhea, say it's probably ulcerative colitis."**
> UC ulcerates the **mucosa**, which is where the surface vessels are. Crohn's inflames deeper but doesn't strip the surface the same way.

### THE COMPARISON

| | **CROHN'S** | **ULCERATIVE COLITIS** |
|---|---|---|
| **Layers** | **ALL layers** | **Mucosal and submucosal only** |
| **Location** | **Distal ileum + ascending colon** | **Colon and rectum**, starting in the rectum |
| **Pattern** | **SKIP lesions**, cobblestone | **CONTINUOUS** lesions |
| **Pain** | **RLQ**, unrelieved by defecation, crampy after meals | **LLQ** |
| **Stool** | Diarrhea | **BLOODY or purulent** diarrhea |

**Why IBD patients become ANEMIC:** the GI lining is **damaged**, so there is **less surface area** for absorption — including iron and B12. Plus **blood loss** in UC, and **food avoidance** in Crohn's.

### Health history — nine things

Onset, duration and characteristics of **pain** · diarrhea · urgency · **tenesmus** · nausea · anorexia · weight loss · **bleeding** · **family history**
Plus dietary patterns, alcohol/caffeine/nicotine use, bowel elimination patterns and stool, and abdominal assessment

### Four complications and four goals

**COMPLICATIONS** — **electrolyte imbalance** · **cardiac dysrhythmias** · **GI bleeding** with fluid loss · **perforation** of the bowel

**GOALS** — normal bowel elimination patterns · relief of abdominal pain and cramping · prevention of **fluid deficit** · maintenance of optimal nutrition

### Medical management (TEXTBOOK)

**INDUCTION** = getting into remission. **MAINTENANCE** = preventing flare-ups.

| Situation | Drug |
|---|---|
| **Mild-moderate UC, first-line** | **MESALAMINE** (aminosalicylate), usually **enema or rectal suppository**. Remission in 4–6 weeks, then tapered |
| **Mild-moderate CROHN'S** | **BUDESONIDE** — oral, enteric-coated corticosteroid, tapered over 8–12 weeks. Aminosalicylates are **less effective** in Crohn's |
| **When those fail** | Systemic corticosteroids — oral **prednisone** or IV **hydrocortisone** |
| **SEVERE disease** | IV corticosteroids **+ immunomodulator + anti-TNF agent** |
| **Crohn's with perianal fistulas** | **Metronidazole + ciprofloxacin** |

> ⚠️ **DO NOT give LOPERAMIDE or DIPHENOXYLATE-ATROPINE for the diarrhea — they can precipitate TOXIC MEGACOLON.** This is the tested point. Antidiarrheals slow a colon that is already inflamed and at risk of dilating.

> **Budesonide's action is mostly enteric/colonic**, which limits the systemic steroid effects of prednisone. Steroid effects still apply: poor wound healing, immunosuppression, bone demineralization.
>
> **Metronidazole** can cause peripheral neuropathy. **Anti-TNF** blocks tumor necrosis factor, an inflammatory signal — the price is infection risk.

### Surgery (TEXTBOOK)

**PROCTOCOLECTOMY** (removing colon and rectum) with **ILEOSTOMY** **CURES UC** — because UC lives **only** in the colon and rectum. Remove them, remove the disease.

**It cannot cure CROHN'S**, because Crohn's can affect **any** part of the GI tract and will recur elsewhere.

**RESTORATIVE PROCTOCOLECTOMY with IPAA** (Ileal Pouch Anal Anastomosis) — colon and rectal mucosa are removed, and a **pouch made from ileum** is connected to the **anus** as a "new rectum." The **anal sphincter is kept**, so voluntary defecation and continence are preserved — **no permanent ileostomy**.

Procedure of choice for severe UC. A **temporary loop ileostomy** protects the anastomosis and is closed about **3 months** later.

> Not recommended for Crohn's, because the disease could recur in the pouch.

## APPENDICITIS

*Not on the blueprint, but referenced three times in lecture and classic NCLEX.*

Inflammation of the **APPENDIX**, a small wormlike appendage on the cecum just below the ileocecal valve. Most commonly from a **FECALITH** — a stone of stool — obstructing it.

> Once obstructed: pressure builds → ischemic → bacteria overgrow → gangrene or perforation.

**Pain behavior:** starts **vague and PERIUMBILICAL** (visceral, dull), then **LOCALIZES to the RLQ** (parietal, sharp) with **rebound tenderness**, low-grade fever, nausea.

**Perforation usually 6–24 hours after onset.**

> She used appendicitis as the example of diffuse-to-localized pain — the same pattern as pancreatitis.

**McBURNEY POINT** — tenderness between the **umbilicus** and the **anterior superior iliac spine** (right hip bone)
**ROVSING SIGN** — pain felt in the **RLQ** when the **LLQ** is palpated

> Rovsing works because pressing the left side pushes gas through the colon toward the inflamed appendix on the right.

**WBC over 10,500** in 80–85% of adults; CT or ultrasound confirms.

### ⚠️ In OLDER ADULTS

Pain may be **absent or minimal** · symptoms **vague** · **fever and leukocytosis may NOT be present**. The patient may have no symptoms until the appendix is **gangrenous or perforated**.

> About 10% of appendectomies are in patients over 60, and complications are higher because diagnosis is delayed. **Age changes how disease presents.**

## ANORECTAL DISORDERS (TEXTBOOK)

*Off the blueprint, but in the assigned chapter.*

**ANAL FISTULA** — a tiny **tubular tract** from the anal canal to an opening in the **perianal skin**. Usually results from an **abscess**; also trauma, fissures, or **Crohn's**. Purulent drainage or **stool leaks constantly**; may pass flatus or feces from the vagina or bladder depending on the tract.
→ **Surgery (fistulotomy)** — few heal on their own. **Recur in up to half** of patients.

**ANORECTAL ABSCESS** — obstruction of an **anal gland** with dried debris → retrograde infection. **Crohn's** and **immunosuppressed** patients are especially susceptible. Dull perianal discomfort, itching, pain **with defecation**; about half have perianal edema. **Most do NOT have fever.**
→ **Incise and drain promptly** — to prevent **fistula formation**, fecal incontinence, and **sepsis**.

> **The sequence is: abscess → fistula.**

**PILONIDAL CYST** — a **"nest of hair"**; hairs penetrate the skin in the **intergluteal cleft** over the lower **sacrum**, forming a cyst with sinus openings. Rarely symptomatic until adolescence or early adulthood, when it infects and forms an abscess.
→ Incised and drained; may be excised later. Wound heals by **granulation** with absorptive dressings.

> *PILO* = hair, *NIDAL* = nest. Classic in young adults who sit a lot.

## DISCRIMINATORS

### Findings SHARED across the lower GI disorders — these separate nothing

Abdominal pain · cramping · bloating and distention · altered bowel pattern · nausea

### The single finding that points to each

| Disorder | The discriminator |
|---|---|
| **IBS** | **NORMAL bowel on every test**; ROME IV criteria |
| **CROHN'S** | **RLQ** pain, **FISTULAS**, skip lesions |
| **ULCERATIVE COLITIS** | **BLOODY diarrhea**, **LLQ** pain, continuous from the rectum |
| **DIVERTICULITIS** | **LLQ cramping with FEVER**, older patient, low-fiber diet |
| **CELIAC** | **STEATORRHEA** that resolves on a gluten-free diet |
| **APPENDICITIS** | Periumbilical pain **MIGRATING to the RLQ**, McBurney point |
