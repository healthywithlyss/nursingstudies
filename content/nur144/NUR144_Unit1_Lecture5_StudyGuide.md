## INDICATIONS AND TYPES

### Four indications for a fecal diversion

**IBD** · **DIVERTICULITIS** · **COLORECTAL CANCER** · **OBSTRUCTION**

**The specialist nurse: the WOC nurse** — Wound, Ostomy and Continence. A **master's-level specialty.**

### Output by location — the one principle

> **The longer contents stay in the large intestine, the more water gets absorbed.**

Everything about ostomy care follows from this.

| Location | Output | Can it be irrigated? |
|---|---|---|
| **ILEOSTOMY** | **CONTINUOUS LIQUID** | **No** |
| **ASCENDING** colostomy | Liquid | No |
| **TRANSVERSE** colostomy | **Soft, UNFORMED** | **No** |
| **DESCENDING or SIGMOID** colostomy | **MORE SOLID** stool | **YES** |

> **NONE have a sphincter**, so none have voluntary control over drainage. All drain to a pouch.

### Volume, onset and consequence

| | **ILEOSTOMY** | **COLOSTOMY** |
|---|---|---|
| **Volume** | **600–1,200 mL** | **200–600 mL** |
| **Onset post-op** | **24–48 hours** | **3–6 days** |
| **Consistency** | Stays **liquid** | More **formed** |

**CONSEQUENCE:** less water absorption means **ELECTROLYTE IMBALANCE** and **HYPOKALEMIA**. Replace **sodium and potassium** as ordered, support with IV fluids.

**Liquid fecal drainage IS counted in output.**

> Everything here follows from one fact: **the ileostomy BYPASSES THE COLON**, so nothing gets reabsorbed. Higher volume, liquid output, faster onset, and the electrolytes leave with the water.
>
> The ileostomy starts sooner because the small bowel wakes up first — the colon takes longer to resume peristalsis after surgery.
>
> Same chain as diarrhea causing dysrhythmias: lose the colon's reabsorption and you lose the electrolytes too.

### CONTINENT ILEOSTOMY (Kock pouch / ileal reservoir)

The **whole colon is removed** and a small **internal pouch** is made from ileum. It **stretches** over time and becomes somewhat continent — the patient **drains it on a routine** once established.

**Initially the drainage is CONTINUOUS for 2–3 weeks.**

> Familial adenomatous polyposis is one reason a young person might have their entire colon removed.
>
> Compare the **IPAA** from Lecture 4 — an ileal pouch connected to the **anus**, keeping the sphincter, so no stoma at all.

## PRE-OPERATIVE CARE

### Seven parts

1. **Fluids** as needed
2. **Blood replacement** as needed
3. **ANTIBIOTICS** as ordered
4. Continue **CORTICOSTEROIDS** as appropriate
5. **LOW RESIDUE, HIGH PROTEIN** diet in small frequent meals
6. **ENSURE THE SURGICAL SITE IS MARKED and VISIBLE TO THE PATIENT**
7. Pre-operative teaching

### Why the site marking matters so much

The **WOC nurse** looks at:
- How the patient **wears their pants**
- Where their **fat folds** are
- A **flat spot** the patient can **SEE and REACH**

Then the patient keeps **re-marking it** until surgery.

> **In a fold, the appliance will not seal** — *"that thing's gonna come off and feces are gonna be everywhere."*
>
> Placement is assessed **sitting AND standing**, because folds appear when seated.
>
> Without marking, the surgeon cuts wherever is easiest **for them**.

> **Antibiotics** because the surgeon is about to cut into the most bacteria-loaded area in the body. Bowel prep reduces the load but cannot eliminate it. Patients often go to the OR with the antibiotic bag **already running**.

## POST-OPERATIVE CARE

### The timeline

**BOWEL SOUNDS** — listen **every shift**. Nothing day 1–2. **GAS day 2–3.**

> **Ask: "Have you passed gas yet?"** Gas is the first sign the bowel woke up.

**OUTPUT** — ileostomy **24–48 hours**; colostomy **3–6 days**

**THE FIRST OUTPUT LOOKS LIKE MECONIUM** — dark and tarry, because it is **shed LINING**, not feces.

> Same reason a newborn's first stool is meconium: mucus and shed lining, no digested food. **Do not mistake it for melena.**

### The rest

- **NG SUCTION** until peristalsis returns. **Nausea and abdominal pain may mean obstruction**
- **RECTAL PACKING** removed in about **1 week** — **medicate for pain 1 HOUR BEFORE**
- **ANTIBIOTICS** from OR through discharge
- **AMBULATION** — for the same reasons as **any** post-op patient: **maintain muscle tone · prevent respiratory infection · prevent blood clots**

> ⚠️ Ambulation is **NOT** to drain the ostomy, and **NOT** related to parenteral nutrition — most ostomy patients don't need PN. Those are classic distractors. **Post-op care first, ostomy-specific care second.**

### PARALYTIC ILEUS

Expected after abdominal surgery. The body doesn't know it was a clean surgical knife — *"it thinks it was gored"* — so the gut **shuts down to protect itself**, keeping contents from moving through an opening into the peritoneal space.

## STOMA ASSESSMENT

| What | Normal | Abnormal |
|---|---|---|
| **COLOR** | **PINK TO BRIGHT RED and SHINY** | **Pale, dusky, purple or black** = compromised blood supply |
| **SWELLING** | Some post-op; **shrinks** over the first weeks | |
| **BLEEDING** | Some oozing | **More than trace**, or **bright red** bleeding |
| **SKIN** | Intact | Any breakdown — **ileostomy has the higher risk** |

> **Pink and shiny** because a stoma is **mucous membrane**, like the inside of your cheek — it should look the same. **Dark means it's losing perfusion, and that is a surgical emergency.**
>
> **The stoma itself has NO nerve endings**, so it does not hurt. **Pain means something around it**, not the stoma.
>
> **Shrinking** is why the template gets re-traced: an appliance cut to last month's size leaves exposed skin, and exposed skin meets **effluent**.

## SKIN AND THE APPLIANCE

### EFFLUENT — why skin breaks down

**EFFLUENT** is the unformed output from a stoma. It contains **ACTIVE DIGESTIVE ENZYMES** that break down food — **and they will break down SKIN just as readily.**

> The GI lining is built to tolerate those enzymes. The skin around the stoma is not. **Any gap between stoma and appliance is skin being digested.**
>
> This is why **ileostomies** — with the most enzyme-rich output — carry the highest skin risk.

### Four causes of skin breakdown

1. **ALLERGIC** reaction to appliance, barrier or paste
2. **CHEMICAL** irritation from **EFFLUENT**
3. **MECHANICAL** injury from removal
4. **INFECTION** — yeast or bacterial

> "Mechanical injury from removal" in practice is usually **it coming off on its own** — nobody removes it for no reason.

### The appliance

**TRACE the stoma** on clear plastic to make a **TEMPLATE**, cut every appliance to those dimensions, and **re-trace as it shrinks**.

**BARRIER PASTE** on the skin first, for extra protection.

**CHANGED every 3–7 DAYS.** **EMPTIED every 4–6 hours**, with voids, or as needed.

> **The goal is the smallest possible gap.**

> **The biggest patient complaint: THE APPLIANCE FALLS OFF** — especially in the first weeks, sometimes in the middle of the night. Insurance often covers only **one change per week**, but early on it needs changing more often.

## IRRIGATION

**What it does:** empties the colon of **GAS, MUCUS and FECES** so the patient can go about social and work activities **without fear of drainage**.

**WHO CAN:** **ONLY** a **DESCENDING or SIGMOID** colostomy

**BEST CANDIDATES:** patients who had a **REGULAR bowel evacuation cycle BEFORE** the stoma

**METHOD:** instill **500–1,000 mL tap water** at the **same time every day** or every other day

### Why only descending or sigmoid?

> **The RISK OF PERFORATION is greater higher up.** Descending and sigmoid are right before the exit — **formed stool, lower pressure, thicker wall.**
>
> Higher up the output is liquid and continuous — **there is nothing to regulate.**

**Also contraindicated with previous PELVIC IRRADIATION**, because radiation causes adhesions and fragile tissue.

> A stoma has no voluntary muscle control and empties at irregular intervals. **Irrigation trains it to a schedule instead.**
>
> Once a routine is established, the patient may switch from a pouch to a **STOMA CAP**. Between irrigations only gas and slight mucus escape. Many patients *"go in the morning and are done for the day."*

## DIET, ODOR AND GAS

### Foods

| **DEODORIZING** | **ODOR-PRODUCING** |
|---|---|
| **SPINACH** | **ASPARAGUS** |
| **PARSLEY** | **CABBAGE** |
| **Bismuth subcarbonate** tablets orally 3–4×/day as ordered | **ONIONS** |
| | **FISH** |

**Also: INCREASE FLUID INTAKE.**

> **Bismuth subcarbonate tablets are like PEPTO-BISMOL and contain ASPIRIN** — so check bleeding risk.

### Gas

Keep a **FOOD DIARY** to find gas-producing foods (**cabbage** is classic). Gas can **inflate the pouch like a balloon and pop it off**; some pouches have a **VENT**.

> The stoma nurses' informal tip: a Tic Tac in the pouch. Not standard practice, but it works.

### ⚠️ THE DEHYDRATION TRAP

**"If nothing goes in, nothing comes out."**

Patients try this. **It causes DEHYDRATION** — her aunt tried it and ended up hospitalized.

> Refusing to eat does **not** stop output. The GI tract sheds lining and secretes fluid regardless — and an ileostomy already loses **600–1,200 mL a day**.
>
> **Fluid intake must go UP, not down.**

## EMOTIONAL SUPPORT

**Expect GRIEF** — altered body function and appearance.

**DELAY EDUCATION until the patient is RECEPTIVE.**

**Address:** **SEXUAL FUNCTION** · **PREGNANCY** · **RECREATION**

**Consult** the WOC nurse and dietitian. **Refer** to the **United Ostomy Associations of America**.

> **Delaying teaching is the counterintuitive one.** A patient in acute grief will not retain it, and pushing damages trust. *"Anybody having this is probably not gonna be OK."*

## QUICK REFERENCE — THE HIGH-YIELD FACTS

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Healthy stoma color | **Pink to bright red, shiny** |
| Stoma color that is an emergency | **Pale, dusky, purple, black** |
| Does the stoma hurt? | **No nerve endings** — pain means something around it |
| First output looks like | **Meconium** (shed lining, not feces) |
| Ileostomy output | **600–1,200 mL, liquid, 24–48 hrs** |
| Colostomy output | **200–600 mL, formed, 3–6 days** |
| Which can be irrigated | **Descending and sigmoid ONLY** |
| Why not higher | **Perforation risk**; liquid output has nothing to regulate |
| Irrigation volume | **500–1,000 mL tap water** |
| Appliance changed | **Every 3–7 days** |
| Pouch emptied | **Every 4–6 hours** |
| Electrolyte at risk | **POTASSIUM** (and sodium) |
| First sign the bowel woke up | **Passing GAS** |
| Why ambulate | Muscle tone, **prevent respiratory infection**, **prevent clots** |
| When to teach | **When the patient is receptive** — not before |
