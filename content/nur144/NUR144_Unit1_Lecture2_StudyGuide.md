## Hepatic Disorders

*Organized by topic, not by objective. Each disease is one complete block.*

---

## THE ONE IDEA EVERYTHING HANGS ON

Everything absorbed from your gut — nutrients, drugs, toxins, bacteria — drains into **one vein**, the portal vein, and goes to the liver **first**. Before it reaches your heart, your brain, anything.

**The liver is a checkpoint.** It processes what's useful, neutralizes what's dangerous, and only then lets blood continue.

When the liver fails, the checkpoint fails. That is most of this lecture.

---

## ANATOMY AND BLOOD SUPPLY

**Where it sits.** Upper right, behind the ribs. Largest gland in the body. 1,200–1,500 g. Four lobes made of **lobules** (the functional units). It is the **only organ that regenerates** — you can donate part and it grows back.

That regeneration is why hepatitis can fully resolve. And why cirrhosis is different: scar tissue does not regenerate into liver.

**Two blood supplies:**

| Supply | Share | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| **Portal vein** | 80% | Nutrient-rich, oxygen-poor — from stomach, intestines, spleen, pancreas |
| **Hepatic artery** | 20% | Oxygen-rich, straight from the aorta |

They merge inside the liver in leaky capillaries called **sinusoids**, where liver cells pull out what they need and add what they made. Then out through the central veins → hepatic vein → vena cava.

**Why the spleen and pancreas drain here too** — not because they absorb food, but because of what they *produce*. The spleen breaks down old red cells, and the bilirubin and iron need processing. The pancreas makes insulin and glucagon, and the liver is their first target.

> **Critical:** Block the flow through the liver — which is what scar tissue does — and 80% of its blood is still arriving with nowhere to go. Pressure backs up the whole portal system. That is **portal hypertension**, and it comes from this one anatomical fact.
>
> It backs up into **veins**, not arteries, and it does **not** reach the heart. The heart is downstream and gets *less* return.

**Kupffer cells** sit in the sinusoids — the body's most common phagocyte. They eat bacteria that crossed the intestinal wall. In cirrhosis, blood detours around the sinusoids and skips this filter, which is why cirrhotics get spontaneous infections.

## THE EIGHT FUNCTIONS — AND THEIR FAILURES

**Do not memorize these as a list.** Learn each job, then ask what happens when it stops. That gives you every complication in the lecture for free.

| Function | What it does | When it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Glucose** | Stores as glycogen, releases between meals | Fasting **hypoglycemia**; post-meal spikes it can't buffer |
| **Ammonia → urea** | Converts toxic ammonia so kidneys can excrete it | Ammonia reaches the brain → **ENCEPHALOPATHY** |
| **Albumin** | Holds fluid inside blood vessels (oncotic pull) | Fluid leaks out → **ASCITES**, edema |
| **Clotting factors** | Built using vitamin K | **BLEEDING**, prolonged PT |
| **Fat** | Breaks fat into fatty acids and ketones for fuel | Fat accumulates in the liver — fatty liver |
| **Storage** | Vitamins A, B, D, plus iron and copper | Deficiencies |
| **Bile** | Emulsifies fat for digestion | Steatorrhea; fat-soluble vitamins lost |
| **Bilirubin** | Conjugates it so it can leave in bile | Backs up → **JAUNDICE** |
| **Drugs** | Metabolizes them, setting half-life | Drugs accumulate — **doses must be REDUCED** |

**Three corrections worth holding onto:**

- **Albumin and clotting factors are two separate products.** Albumin has nothing to do with clotting.
- **Alcohol does not become ammonia.** Alcohol → acetaldehyde → acetate. Ammonia comes from dietary protein, gut bacteria, and **blood in the GI tract** (blood is protein — which is why a GI bleed tips a cirrhotic into encephalopathy).
- **Vitamin K** comes from gut bacteria and leafy greens. It's fat-soluble, so no bile means it isn't absorbed. The liver then *uses* it to build clotting factors. Liver failure hits clotting twice.

## BILIRUBIN AND JAUNDICE

### Where bilirubin comes from

Red cells live 90–120 days. The **SPLEEN** breaks them down — not the liver — and the heme becomes bilirubin.

| Stage | Form | Solubility | Carrier | Can enter urine? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before liver | **Unconjugated** (indirect) | Fat-soluble | Bound to **albumin** | **No** — too large to pass the kidney filter |
| After liver | **Conjugated** (direct) | **Water-soluble** | None needed | **Yes** |

> "Direct and indirect, conjugated and unconjugated — **two words for the same two things**, not four different things."

### The fork that matters

- **Urea's normal exit** is blood → kidney. By design.
- **Bilirubin's normal exit** is bile → intestine → stool. That's why stool is brown.

**Bilirubin in urine is always abnormal.** It means the normal exit is blocked and it's backing up. And since only conjugated can pass the filter, bilirubin in urine *always* means it already went through the liver.

### The three types of jaundice

Jaundice is a **sign, not a disease** — like saying "they have a virus." Check the **sclera first**; the white background shows yellow earliest, which matters especially in darker skin.

**Visible above 2.0 mg/dL** (textbook). Her slide: normal 0.2–1.3, jaundice above 2.5.

| Type | Where the chain broke | Which bilirubin | Clinical picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hemolytic** | **Before** the liver — cells destroyed faster than a healthy liver can keep up | **Unconjugated** | Weak, dizzy, **pale**; urine **free** of bilirubin |
| **Hepatocellular** | **At** the liver — damaged, can't do either half | **Both** | Anorexia, nausea, weight loss; may be **reversible** |
| **Obstructive** | **After** the liver — exit blocked | **Conjugated** | The four findings below |
| **Fulminant** | Massive sudden cell death | — | Failure within **8 weeks** of jaundice appearing |

### The obstructive four — all from one blocked pipe

Bile isn't reaching the intestine:

1. **Clay-colored stool** — bile pigment is what makes stool brown. No bile, no color.
2. **Dark orange, foamy urine** — conjugated bilirubin is water-soluble, so it exits through the kidneys instead. Foamy because bilirubin lowers surface tension.
3. **Pruritus** — bile salts that should have gone to the gut deposit in skin. They are irritants.
4. **Dyspepsia and fat intolerance** — no bile to emulsify fat.

## LIVER LABS

**The organizing idea:** every lab is either a **DAMAGE marker** (enzymes leaking out of hurt cells) or a **FUNCTION marker** (things the liver should be making and isn't).

| Lab | Type | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **ALT** | Damage | **Liver-specific.** If it's up, it's the liver |
| **AST** | Damage | **Not specific** — also heart, muscle, kidney. Isolated AST doesn't localize |
| **GGT / GGTP** | Damage | Rises with cholestasis and alcohol; confirms a high ALP is hepatic |
| **LDH** | Damage | Nonspecific cell damage |
| **Alkaline phosphatase** | Obstruction | Made in **bile duct lining** — rises when bile flow is blocked |
| **Albumin** | **Function** | Liver makes it → falls in chronic disease → ascites and edema |
| **A/G ratio** | Function | **Reverses** — albumin falls while globulins rise |
| **PT / INR** | **Function** | Liver makes prothrombin → prolonged = it has stopped. A **synthetic** marker |
| **Ammonia** | Function | The toxin in encephalopathy; checked daily once suspected |
| **Cholesterol** | Mixed | **Up** in biliary obstruction, **down** in parenchymal disease |

> If a value is high, you may need to **limit which medications you give** — the liver cannot clear them.

### Liver biopsy

**Before:** informed consent · **PT, PTT, platelets** (you're puncturing an organ in a patient who may not clot) · compatible blood available · baseline vitals · explain the breath-hold.

**Position FOR the procedure:** supine or **left lateral** — brings the liver forward against the ribs.

**Position AFTER:** **RIGHT side**, several hours — presses the liver against the rib cage and seals the puncture.

**After:** no coughing or straining · vitals q10–15 min then q30 · watch for bleeding, shock, and abdominal rigidity (blood or bile in the peritoneum → peritonitis, the most common complication) · **no heavy lifting for 1 week.**

> Left for the needle, right for the healing. Students mix these up.

## HEPATITIS

**Inflammation of liver cells with necrosis.** *-itis* means inflammation — **not** infection. A virus can cause it, but so can a toxin, a drug, alcohol, or autoimmune attack.

- **Infectious causes:** viruses A, B, C, D, E (plus G and GB virus-C)
- **Toxic causes:** chemicals and poisons, and **drug-induced**

**Drug-induced is over 50% of acute liver failure**, with **acetaminophen** the leading cause. The liver metabolizes it into a toxic byproduct; at high doses it runs out of the antioxidant needed to neutralize it. May reverse if stopped early, or require transplant. **Isoniazid** resembles viral hepatitis at onset.

### The three phases — MUST KNOW

All types of hepatitis have all three.

**PRE-ICTERIC** — 1–3 weeks before jaundice
Fatigue, anorexia, flu-like, low-grade fever, **joint pain**, **changes in taste and smell**, weight loss. AST/ALT and urinary bilirubin already rising.
*The "run down and don't know why" phase.*

**ICTERIC** — 2–6 weeks
Begins with **jaundice**. RUQ discomfort (the swollen liver stretches its capsule). Dark urine, clay stool, pruritus — the obstructive picture, because the inflamed liver blocks its own ducts. Direct bilirubin up.

**POST-ICTERIC** — 2–12 weeks
Recovering, jaundice fading, but **continued malaise and easy fatigue** while the liver rebuilds.

> Even after full recovery, **titers stay positive** — some patients can never donate blood.

### The five viruses

**Group first.** Two routes:

- **Fecal-oral: A and E** — contaminated food, water, hands
- **Blood and sexual: B, C, D** — needles, transfusion, sex, and for B, birth

| | A | B | C | D | E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Route** | Fecal-oral | Blood/sexual/**birth** | Blood | Blood — **needs B** | Fecal-oral |
| **Incubation** | 15–50 days | **1–6 months** | — | — | 15–65 days |
| **Vaccine** | **Yes** | **Yes** | **NO** | Prevent B | No |
| **Chronic?** | **No** | Sometimes | **Yes — usually** | Yes | **No** |
| **The one fact** | No carrier state | First shot a newborn gets | No vaccine; alcohol accelerates it | Cannot exist without B | **Severe in pregnancy** (up to 20% mortality 3rd trimester) |

**More detail worth having:**

- **B** — 90% of infected *newborns* become chronic carriers vs. ~5% of adults. Long-term → cirrhosis and liver cancer. Treated with alpha interferon and antivirals.
- **C** — most common bloodborne infection in the US. No vaccine because it mutates too fast. **Direct-acting antivirals cure over 90%** now. Leading reason for transplant.
- **D** — an *incomplete* virus; needs B's outer coat to replicate. Co-infection (both at once) or superinfection (D on top of chronic B, worse). Treated with interferon alfa.

**Active vs. passive immunity:** a **vaccine** is active — your body makes the antibodies, slow but lasting. **Immunoglobulin** is passive — someone else's antibodies, immediate but temporary, given to an exposed contact.

## CIRRHOSIS

Hepatitis is inflammation. **Cirrhosis is what repeated or chronic inflammation becomes:** the liver repairs itself with **scar tissue**. Scar does not do liver work, and it does not let blood through. **Irreversible.**

### Three types

| Type | Scar pattern | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Alcoholic** | Surrounds the **portal areas** | Chronic alcohol use — **most common in the US** |
| **Postnecrotic** | **Broad bands** | Late result of previous acute viral hepatitis |
| **Biliary** | Around the **bile ducts** | Chronic biliary obstruction and cholangitis — **least common** |

> **Note:** "Postnecrotic" describes a *mechanism* — after necrosis — not a cause. Drug-induced hepatitis, toxins and autoimmune hepatitis produce the same pattern. The textbook names viral hepatitis because it's historically most common. Answer the textbook on the exam.

### Compensated vs. decompensated — it is CUMULATIVE

**COMPENSATED** — the liver is damaged but **still performing**. Vague signs, often found incidentally:

Intermittent mild fever · firm **enlarged** liver with sharp palpable edge · abdominal pain · flatulent dyspepsia · **ankle edema** · **palmar erythema** · **vascular spiders** · **splenomegaly** · **epistaxis**

**DECOMPENSATED — everything above, PLUS:**

*What worsens:*
- Intermittent fever → **continuous**
- Ankle edema → **ASCITES**
- Enlarged liver → **SHRINKS and becomes nodular**
- Epistaxis → **purpura, petechiae, spontaneous bruising**

*What's newly added:*
Jaundice · clubbing · **white nails** · **muscle wasting**, weakness, weight loss · hypotension · gonadal atrophy, sparse body hair, **gynecomastia**, **amenorrhea** · **caput medusae** · nausea, vomiting, anorexia · **hypokalemia and hyponatremia** · **asterixis** and mental status change · **thrombocytopenia**

**Derive them, don't memorize:**

| Sign | Failed function |
|---|---|
| Ascites, white nails | No albumin |
| Jaundice | Bilirubin not excreted |
| Bruising, purpura | No clotting factors + platelets trapped in congested spleen |
| Spider angiomas, palmar erythema, gynecomastia, amenorrhea | **Estrogen** the liver can't inactivate |
| Muscle wasting | No glycogen storage → body catabolizes its own muscle |
| Asterixis, mental status | Ammonia not converted |
| Caput medusae, splenomegaly | Portal pressure backing up |
| Hypokalemia | Aldosterone the liver can't metabolize + diuretics |
| Hyponatremia | **Dilutional** — water retained faster than sodium (restrict water, don't give salt) |

> **Liver size changes over the disease.** Large and firm early (cells swollen with fat), small and nodular late (scar contracts). **A small liver is worse than a large one.**

> **The three life-threatening markers:** ASCITES · ENCEPHALOPATHY · VARICEAL HEMORRHAGE

> **She listed MENTAL STATUS CHANGE first** among complication signs — it's the one you assess with no equipment.

## THE CHAIN

> **ALCOHOL → SCARRING → blood cannot pass → PORTAL HYPERTENSION → pressure backs into GI veins → veins BULGE → VARICES**

Every arrow is a cause. Know the chain and you can reason through any question about it.

## ASCITES

Fluid collecting in the **peritoneal space** — outside the bowels, inside the abdomen.

### Four mechanisms

1. Portal pressure forcing fluid out
2. Splanchnic vasodilation
3. **Aldosterone** the liver can't metabolize → salt and water retention
4. **Low albumin** → nothing holds fluid in the vessels

### Third spacing — the concept

- **First space** — inside blood vessels; the fluid actually circulating
- **Second space** — interstitial, between cells; fluid moves here normally
- **Third space** — anywhere fluid collects that it **can't easily return from**: peritoneal cavity, pleural space, bowel lumen, burned tissue

> **The paradox:** a patient with ten liters of ascites is **hypovolemic**. The fluid is there — it's just not circulating.

**Albumin** is too big to fit through capillary pores, so it stays in the vessels and attracts water — **oncotic pressure**. In cirrhosis the liver stops making it, the pull weakens, and fluid leaks out. **Giving IV albumin puts the magnet back**, drawing fluid from the third space into circulation.

Same physics appears in: **dumping syndrome** (gut lumen), **perforation** (peritoneum), **bowel obstruction** (distended loops).

### Assessment — seven things

**Abdominal girth** at the same landmark serially (it develops slowly) · **daily weights** (a kilo overnight is a liter) · striae, distended veins, umbilical hernia · **dull percussion** where it should be hollow · **fluid wave** (tap one flank, feel it arrive at the other — only free fluid transmits it) · I&O · electrolytes

### Treatment — five, each with its reason

| Treatment | Why |
|---|---|
| **Low sodium** | Water follows salt |
| **Potassium-sparing diuretics** (spironolactone) | Blocks **aldosterone** the liver can't metabolize; potassium-sparing because they're already hypokalemic and a loop diuretic would worsen it |
| **IV albumin** | The liver stopped making it; replacing it pulls fluid back into circulation |
| **Paracentesis** | Physically drains it when it impairs breathing |
| **TIPS** | Bypasses the block; second-line, costs encephalopathy risk |

### Paracentesis — five nursing responsibilities

1. **Position upright** — fluid pools low where the cannula can reach; bowel loops stay away from the needle
2. **Vital signs frequently** — heart rate rises before pressure falls
3. **Monitor for hypovolemia and mental status change**
4. **Assess the puncture site** — they don't clot, and ascitic fluid keeps leaking
5. **Record the amount** — it determines albumin dosing

> **The danger is the fluid shift.** Drain several liters and the body refills the space by pulling from the bloodstream. The patient who looked bloated goes into hypovolemic shock. **That's exactly why albumin is given** — it holds fluid in the vessels so it can't be recruited.

## ESOPHAGEAL VARICES

Veins in the esophagus **swollen and bulged** because portal blood is forced through them. Thin-walled, easily ruptured, and sitting where food scrapes past.

### Incidence and mortality

- Bleeding occurs in **1/3** of patients with cirrhosis and varices
- **First bleed mortality: SLIDE says 30–50%. Textbook says 10–30%.** *Use her number.*
- Varices present in 30% of **compensated** cirrhosis, 60% of **decompensated**
- **70% rebleed within one year** — which is why banding and beta blockers continue
- **Screening endoscopy every 2 years** — find them before the first bleed

### Assessment

**Hematemesis** — vomit stained **RED** (esophageal) or **BLACK** (gastric)
**Melena** — black tarry stools (gastric)
**General deterioration and shock** from loss of fluid and blood

> The color tells you where. Blood from an **esophageal** varix comes up immediately and is still red. Blood from a **gastric** varix sits in stomach acid first, which turns it black.

> **Dark tarry stool is more concerning than bright red on the paper.** The darker it is, the further it traveled and the higher the source.

### Treatment

**Endoscopic:**
- **Sclerotherapy** — a chemical injected into the varix irritates the wall, causing thrombosis and sclerosis. Obliterated from inside.
- **Banding** — a rubber band cuts off blood supply; the tissue dies and sloughs in days. *Like a string around a dog's tail.*
- Afterward, **angiogenesis** builds new collaterals. Blood always finds a way around; treatment removes the *dangerous* routes.

**For active bleeding:** oxygen (less blood means less hemoglobin — maximize what's left) · IV fluids and electrolytes · **volume expanders** (albumin, hetastarch — they stay in vessels longer because they carry oncotic pull) · blood products per H&H

**Medications:**

| Drug | When | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **Propranolol, nadolol, carvedilol** | **Prevention** — lower portal pressure | **THE TRAP: they blunt the tachycardia that is your earliest bleeding sign.** A beta-blocked cirrhotic can hemorrhage with a normal pulse |
| **Vasopressin** | Active bleeding — constricts splanchnic vessels | **Angina** — nitroglycerin may run alongside |
| **Octreotide** | Active bleeding | Preferred — no coronary effect |

### Balloon tamponade — nursing priorities

- **Suction working, tube not clogged** — if it clogs, blood accumulates above the balloon and is aspirated
- **INTERMITTENT, never continuous** — continuous pulls the tube against mucosa and creates its own ulcer
- **Respiratory status** — the esophageal balloon sits just below the airway; if it migrates up it can obstruct. **Scissors at the bedside**
- **Auscultate BOTH lungs** — aspirated blood doesn't always go to one side
- Monitor for hypovolemic shock

## HYPOVOLEMIC SHOCK — KNOW THE ORDER

The highest-yield sequence in the lecture.

| Order | Sign | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **HEART RATE RISES** | Baroreceptors sense the volume drop; sympathetic system compensates by beating faster. This works for a while — which is why pressure holds |
| **2** | **Urine output falls** | Kidneys take 20–25% of cardiac output; they feel it before pressure changes |
| 3 | Rapid breathing | Tissues go anaerobic → lactic acid → blow off CO2. Plus sympathetic drive |
| 4 | Cold clammy skin, pallor, diaphoresis | Blood shunted from skin to vital organs |
| 5 | Anxiety | Brain underperfused, sympathetic firing |
| **6** | **BLOOD PRESSURE FALLS** | Only when compensation fails |
| 7 | Weak thready pulse, unconsciousness | |

> **"If you let it get to blood pressure, you didn't do a good job monitoring the heart rate."**

> **H&H can look NORMAL early** in an acute bleed — plasma and cells are lost together, so concentration hasn't changed yet.

## HEPATIC ENCEPHALOPATHY

Brain dysfunction from toxins the failing liver can no longer clear — chiefly **ammonia**.

**Where ammonia comes from:** dietary protein breaking down · gut bacteria digesting protein in the colon · **blood in the GI tract** (blood is protein — which is why a GI bleed tips a cirrhotic into encephalopathy)

**Why TIPS makes it worse:** shunted blood reaches the body **without passing the checkpoint**.

### The four stages

| Stage | Findings | EEG |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Lethargy, euphoria, **reversed day-night sleep**, **impaired handwriting**, asterixis | **NORMAL** |
| **2** | Drowsiness, disorientation, inappropriate behavior, labile mood, agitation, **fetor hepaticus** | Slowing |
| **3** | Stuporous, hard to arouse, incoherent, **INCREASED reflexes, RIGIDITY** | Markedly abnormal |
| **4** | **COMATOSE**, may not respond to pain. **ASTERIXIS ABSENT. Reflexes ABSENT, FLACCID** | — |

> **THE TRAP: rigid with increased reflexes is stage 3; flaccid with none is stage 4. ABSENCE IS WORSE.**
>
> In stage 3 the brain has lost inhibitory control over spinal reflexes, so they fire unchecked. In stage 4 the cord itself is depressed. A question offering "absent reflexes" as the milder finding is testing exactly this.

> **EEG is normal at stage 1** — which is why a **daily handwriting sample** is the monitoring tool. Fine motor change appears before anything measurable.

### Assessment — seven ways

Mental status (earliest, free) · asterixis · fetor hepaticus · **daily handwriting sample** · ammonia level · EEG · electrolytes (**hypokalemia and alkalosis both increase ammonia crossing into the brain**) · I&O

### Treatment

**LACTULOSE** — the centerpiece. It's a sugar humans can't digest. Gut bacteria ferment it into acids, which convert ammonia (NH₃, absorbable) into **ammonium** (NH₄⁺, trapped). The osmotic pull carries it out as stool.

> **Titrate to 2–3 soft stools a day.** More is overdose — watery diarrhea, dehydration, hypokalemia (which itself worsens encephalopathy). **No other laxatives** — they confuse the titration.

**IV GLUCOSE** — without glucose the body breaks down its own protein for fuel, producing **more** ammonia. Glucose prevents that catabolism.

**ANTIBIOTICS** — neomycin, metronidazole, rifaximin. Kill the gut bacteria making ammonia.

**STOP sedatives, tranquilizers, analgesics** — three reasons: the liver can't clear them so they accumulate · the brain is already depressed and they can tip stage 2 into stage 4 · they mask mental status, your primary monitor. **Keep the patient active during the day** to restore the sleep-wake cycle instead.

### ⚠️ THE PROTEIN CONFLICT

**Her slide says RESTRICT. The textbook says DO NOT — maintain 1.2–1.5 g/kg/day.**

Both reasonings are sound:
- Less protein → less ammonia *(why restriction was traditional)*
- But restrict too far and the body catabolizes its **own muscle**, which is protein — producing **more** ammonia plus wasting

**Branched-chain amino acids** are the compromise — usable by muscle without liver processing.

The real goal is minimizing ammonia **formation and absorption**, which lactulose and antibiotics do. **Ask her which answer she wants.**

## NURSING CARE — EVERY INTERVENTION WITH ITS WHY

### Bleeding risk

**Two separate reasons:** no clotting factors (liver makes them using vitamin K — and vitamin K is fat-soluble so no bile means it isn't absorbed either) **and** thrombocytopenia (portal hypertension congests the spleen, which traps and destroys platelets).

- **Electric razor** — a nick that wouldn't matter in anyone else bleeds persistently
- **Soft toothbrush** — gums bleed at every brushing
- **Extended pressure** on venipuncture sites
- **Evaluate ANY injury for internal bleeding** — a fall that would bruise a normal patient can cause a retroperitoneal or intracranial bleed with nothing to stop it

### Skin and pruritus

Turn **q2h** (edema and poor nutrition make skin fragile) · gentle care, no harsh soap or hot water · **emollients** (dry skin itches more) · **CHOLESTYRAMINE** — binds bile salts in the gut so they're excreted instead of depositing in skin; treats the *cause* · nails short · **rub, don't scratch**

> The biggest complication of scratching is **secondary infection** in a patient who can't fight it.

### Nutrition

- **Water-soluble forms of A, D, E, K** — with steatorrhea the fat-soluble versions are trapped in undigested fat and lost
- **High calorie** — no glycogen storage, so steady intake prevents catabolizing their own protein
- **Small meals with supplements** — ascites presses on the stomach
- **Reduce sodium** if ascites — water follows salt
- **NO ALCOHOL** — it's the cause; the single most important intervention
- **NO RAW SHELLFISH** — *Vibrio vulnificus* causes fatal sepsis specifically in cirrhotics, because the Kupffer cells that would clear it are bypassed

### Activity intolerance

Rest (reduces metabolic demand) · position for respiratory efficiency (ascites pushes the diaphragm up) · oxygen (they're anemic) · mild exercise with rest periods · adequate nutrition · prevent hazards

> Same set applies to anemia, heart failure and COPD. Learn it once.

### Safety

**Fall risk** and **can't be trusted to remember their regimen**, because encephalopathy affects the brain. Assess the home. **Periodic neuro checks** — encephalopathy fluctuates, so a patient clear this morning may be confused tonight. No driving or machinery. Home visits if they live alone.

### Complementary therapies — ALWAYS ASK

Patients don't consider supplements to be drugs and won't volunteer them.

- **KAVA KAVA** — directly **hepatotoxic**; documented acute liver failure requiring transplant
- **MILK THISTLE** — taken *for* the liver (silymarin), weak evidence, and interacts with medications the liver is already struggling to clear

## THE TEXTBOOK EXTRAS

Not on her slides, but in the assigned chapters.

**Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis** — bacterial peritonitis in a cirrhotic with ascites, **no perforation**. Ascitic fluid becomes a culture medium and the Kupffer filter is bypassed. **Precipitates encephalopathy.**

**Liver transplant** — for end-stage disease. Priority by **MELD score**, not time on the list.

**Child-Pugh classification** — severity scoring using five parameters: **bilirubin, albumin, prothrombin time, ascites, encephalopathy.** (Two synthetic markers, one excretory, two clinical complications.)

**Liver abscess** — **amebic** (*Entamoeba histolytica*, tropical) or **pyogenic** (bacterial).

**NAFLD / NASH** — lipids accumulating in hepatocytes. Unrelated to alcohol = NAFLD; with inflammation = **NASH** (steatohepatitis). Associated with obesity and metabolic syndrome; can progress to cirrhosis.

**Gilbert syndrome** — familial, mild jaundice from increased **unconjugated** bilirubin, liver otherwise normal. **Benign.** The answer when isolated unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia appears with everything else normal.

**Hepatocellular carcinoma** — associated with **cirrhosis and hepatitis B/C**. The chain: chronic infection → chronic inflammation → cirrhosis → cancer.

## ⚠️ CONFLICTS TO TAKE TO THE PROFESSOR
